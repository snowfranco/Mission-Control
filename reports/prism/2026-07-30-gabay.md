# Prism Memo: Gabay regulatory-tailwind thesis (HB 8101 + DMW enforcement)

## Verdict
Park the thesis. Extend existing: Gabay stays slot 3 (validating) on its current plan; do not reprioritize or accelerate on this regulatory story.

In 3 sentences: The regulatory "tailwind" is weaker than the card frames it, because HB 8101 is a bill only just filed (Feb 25, 2026), not a law, and its central mechanism is a government-built free claims portal that could compete with Gabay's paid claims workflow rather than create pull for it. DMW anti-illegal-recruitment enforcement is real and active, but it drives free verification against the DMW's own public list, which is near-zero willingness to pay. The thesis does not establish decision-grade commercial pull, so it should not substitute for Gabay's own demand experiment (the existing next_gate).

## Path to revenue
This memo cannot write a Go-grade path because the thesis does not create one. Documented model (registry commercial_line; ofw_legal_ai_foundational_docs.md): freemium query packs, document-generation fees (demand letters, complaint affidavits, OWWA claim forms), and lawyer referral fees. Slot 3 target is $1,500 MRR (portfolio.yaml). The problem: verification, the feature the regulation and enforcement actually push demand toward, is free on the DMW website, so it is a funnel feature, not a revenue feature. Revenue must come from document generation and referrals, none of which HB 8101 or DMW warnings directly create demand for. If HB 8101 passes, the mandated free Electronic OFW Claims Portal would handle claims filing at $0, undercutting the paid claims-workflow revenue line. Who pays and how much remains unvalidated; that is exactly what Gabay's unrun demand experiment is meant to answer.

## Intersect memo (related_projects: [gabay])
Extension, not net-new, and it should change little in Gabay's ROADMAP. The correct move is to hold the line: keep Gabay at slot 3 validating, write the consumer-validation completion criteria (the PARKING_LOT.md flag and current next_gate), and run the demand experiment before any regulatory narrative is used to justify build acceleration. What would be wrong: treating HB 8101 as validated demand and pulling Gabay forward. The regulation is a monitor signal, not a trigger. If HB 8101 advances past committee, re-open this as a fresh card (it changes the competitive picture, in both directions).

## Evidence
- HB 8101 "OFW Claims Assurance Act of 2026" was filed Feb 25, 2026 by Reps Jocelyn and Ralph Tulfo, 20th Congress First Regular Session; it is introduced, not enacted (docs.congress.hrep.online/legisdocs/basic_20/HB08101.pdf; facebook.com/jocelyntulfoinaction post). Label: evidence. Torus's card implies the portal exists ("creates an Electronic OFW Claims Portal"); that is inference/overstatement, the bill only mandates DMW to build one within 180 days of effectivity if passed.
- The bill mandates a centralized government Electronic OFW Claims Portal as the "primary and mandatory" filing platform, free to OFWs (HB08101.pdf, Sec. on Portal). Label: evidence. Inference: a free mandated government portal competes with, not feeds, Gabay's paid claims workflow.
- DMW enforcement is real and active: Feb 5, 2026 news release on anti-illegal-recruitment; repeated 2026 public warnings to verify agencies (pna.gov.ph/articles/1274277; gmanetwork.com 986364); agency shutdowns in Legazpi and Manila (facebook.com/PIAROV; facebook.com/dmwphl). Label: evidence.
- Verification is free and self-service on the DMW site (dmw.gov.ph/inquiry/licensed-recruitment-agencies; respicio.ph how-to-check commentary). Label: evidence. Inference: a wrapper over a free government lookup has near-zero standalone willingness to pay.

## Kill conditions
1. Demand experiment shows OFWs will not pay for document generation or referrals once verification is free (kills the revenue model, not just this thesis).
2. HB 8101 passes and DMW ships the free claims portal, directly displacing Gabay's paid claims workflow.
3. DMW or a partner NGO launches a free rights-guidance chatbot, occupying the free-information layer Gabay relies on for acquisition.
4. Paid conversion in the demand experiment lands below the (still-unwritten) completion threshold.
5. Tagalog legal-answer accuracy cannot clear the safety bar for a consumer legal product, blocking launch regardless of demand.

## Cost of being wrong
If Park is wrong (real pull exists now and we wait): low cost, because Gabay is early-stage validating with no pricing set and the demand experiment is the gating step anyway; we lose little by not accelerating on an unpassed bill. If we had instead gone Go on this thesis and it is wrong: we would burn build cycles on a claims workflow that a free government portal may obviate, and spend the scarce commercial-slot attention on a regulatory story instead of the demand test that actually de-risks the bet. Parking is the cheaper error.

## Slot math
No change. Gabay keeps slot 3 (validating). Parking this thesis does not free or fill a slot; it declines to accelerate. WIP cap (5) and commercial cap (3) unchanged. No displacement proposed.
